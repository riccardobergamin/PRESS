BCOS: transport with killing and competing risks

This appendix develops a model of deterministic transport with interior killing and boundary absorption, and describes its application to BCOS in PRESS. The forward equation for diffusion with killing and absorption studied by Holcman, Marchewka and Schuss (Holcman, Marchewka, and Schuss 2005) motivates the mathematical structure. Here, deterministic transport is combined with a probabilistic reference law and competing terminal events.

1. Reference transport model

Consider a particle on \((-\infty,L)\), with absorption at \(L>0\). Conditional on its initial position, its motion is deterministic until killing or absorption. Let \(\tau:(-\infty,L]\to\mathbb R\) be continuous, strictly increasing, locally absolutely continuous, with \(\tau'>0\) almost everywhere and \(\tau(\ell)\to-\infty\) as \(\ell\to-\infty\). Define the transport velocity almost everywhere by

\[ \nu(\ell)=1/\tau'(\ell). \]

For \(\kappa>0\), introduce the logistic reference function

\[ F(\ell,t)=[1+\exp\{-\kappa[t-\tau(\ell)]\}]^{-1}. \]

It is the probability that the particle has reached \(\ell\) by time \(t\) in the absence of killing; the particle reaches \(\ell\) at time \(\tau(\ell)+T\), with \(T\) a logistic delay common to all positions. The corresponding density of the particle’s position, \(p_0\), and its current are

\[ \begin{aligned} p_0(\ell,t)&=-\partial_\ell F(\ell,t)=\kappa\tau'(\ell)F(\ell,t)[1-F(\ell,t)],\\ \nu(\ell)p_0(\ell,t)&=\partial_tF(\ell,t)=\kappa F(\ell,t)[1-F(\ell,t)]. \end{aligned} \]

Equality of the mixed derivatives, in the distributional sense, gives

\[ \partial_tp_0+\partial_\ell(\nu p_0)=0. \]

2. Initial conditioning

The initial condition is the reference density at time zero, conditioned on non-absorption. At \(t=0\), the reference law assigns mass \(F(L,0)\) to the destination or beyond; removing it and retaining all mass on \((-\infty,L)\) gives

\[ Z=1-F(L,0),\qquad p_k(\ell,0)=p_0(\ell,0)/Z,\quad -\infty<\ell<L. \]

Since \(F(-\infty,0)=1\), the initial density integrates to one. The reference law has median at the point where \(\tau(\ell)=0\), when that point exists. In particular, if \(\tau(0)=0\), its unconditioned median is zero; truncation at \(L\) shifts the conditioned median. The negative half-line can represent a latent waiting state, as in the BCOS application below.

3. Transport with killing

Let \(k(\ell,t)\ge0\) be measurable and bounded on the domain over each finite time interval. The surviving density satisfies

\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \partial_tp_k+\partial_\ell(\nu p_k)&=-kp_k, &&\ell<L,\\ p_k(\ell,0)&=p_0(\ell,0)/Z. \end{aligned}} \]

The absorbing outflow boundary \(L\) is the only finite boundary of the domain, and the density there is determined by the transport. The current vanishes at \(-\infty\), as follows from the characteristic solution below.

4. Solution by characteristics

Use the transport-time coordinate \(\zeta=\tau(\ell)-\tau(0)\). The density in this coordinate is the current \(J_k=\nu p_k\), since \(\mathrm d\ell=\nu\,\mathrm d\zeta\) almost everywhere. The conservative equation becomes

\[ \partial_tJ_k+\partial_\zeta J_k=-kJ_k, \]

where \(k\) is evaluated at \(\ell=\tau^{-1}(\zeta+\tau(0))\). The characteristic ending at \((\ell,t)\) is

\[ X_\xi=\tau^{-1}(\tau(\ell)-t+\xi),\qquad 0\le\xi\le t. \]

Every backward characteristic reaches the initial line because \(\tau\) tends to \(-\infty\) at the left end. This construction also specifies the passage through points where the spatial velocity vanishes: transport proceeds at unit speed in \(\zeta\) through such points.

Along a characteristic,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\xi}J_k(X_\xi,\xi)=-k(X_\xi,\xi)J_k(X_\xi,\xi). \]

The reference function and current are constant along characteristics because \(\xi-\tau(X_\xi)=t-\tau(\ell)\). The initial current is therefore \(\partial_tF(\ell,t)/Z\). Integration yields

\[ \boxed{p_k(\ell,t)=\frac{p_0(\ell,t)}{Z}w(\ell,t),\qquad w(\ell,t)=\exp\!\left[-\int_0^t k(X_\xi,\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi\right].} \]

The weight \(w\) is the probability of surviving killing along the entire characteristic since time zero. Since \(0\le w\le1\), the current \(J_k=\partial_tF\,w/Z\) vanishes as \(\ell\to-\infty\). Without killing, \(p_k=p_0/Z\).

5. Competing risks

The terminal fluxes are

\[ \boxed{j_L(t)=\nu(L)p_k(L^-,t),\qquad j_k(t)=\int_{-\infty}^L k(\ell,t)p_k(\ell,t)\,\mathrm{d} \ell.} \]

Integration of the transport equation over \((-\infty,L)\) gives

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int_{-\infty}^L p_k(\ell,t)\,\mathrm d\ell= -j_L(t)-j_k(t). \]

The total at-risk probability is the surviving mass:

\[ \boxed{S(t)=\int_{-\infty}^L p_k(\ell,t)\,\mathrm d\ell.} \]

With \(C_L\) and \(C_k\) denoting cumulative incidences of absorption and of killing, respectively,

\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} S'&=-j_L-j_k, & S(0)&=1,\\ C_L'&=j_L, & C_L(0)&=0,\\ C_k'&=j_k, & C_k(0)&=0. \end{aligned}} \]

Adding the equations yields \(S(t)+C_L(t)+C_k(t)=1\). These balances hold almost everywhere in time under the stated assumptions.

For \(S(t)>0\), the cause-specific hazards are

\[ \boxed{h_L(t)=\frac{j_L(t)}{S(t)},\qquad h_k(t)=\frac{j_k(t)}{S(t)}.} \]

The total hazard is \(h_L+h_k=-S'/S\), and

\[ \begin{aligned} S(t)&=\exp\!\left[-\int_0^t [h_L(\xi)+h_k(\xi)]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right],\\ C_L(t)&=\int_0^t h_L(\xi)S(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad C_k(t)=\int_0^t h_k(\xi)S(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi. \end{aligned} \]

Bounded killing on finite time intervals implies \(S(t)>0\) at every finite time.

Multiple killing causes

Suppose killing can occur through several competing causes indexed by \(i\), with local intensities \(k_i(\ell,t)\). Conditional on the particle’s trajectory, assume independent killing processes, so that

\[ k(\ell,t)=\sum_i k_i(\ell,t). \]

Along a characteristic, the survival weight factorizes as

\[ w(\ell,t)=\prod_{i}\exp\!\left[-\int_{0}^t k_i(X_\xi,\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi\right]. \]

The additive sink partitions the killing flux into mutually exclusive terminal causes:

\[ j_i(t)=\int_{-\infty}^L k_i(\ell,t)p_k(\ell,t)\,\mathrm{d}\ell,\qquad j_k(t)=\sum_i j_i(t). \]

The cause-specific hazards and cumulative incidences are

\[ h_i(t)=\frac{j_i(t)}{S(t)},\qquad C_i(t)=\int_0^t S(\xi)h_i(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi. \]

Consequently \(S(t)+C_L(t)+\sum_i C_i(t)=1\). The individual fluxes depend on all killing intensities through the surviving density \(p_k\).

6. Terminal probabilities

Since \(0\le w\le1\), we have \(0\le S(t)\le [1-F(L,t)]/Z\to0\). Consequently, the terminal probabilities satisfy

\[ C_L(\infty)=\int_0^\infty j_L(t)\,\mathrm{d} t,\qquad C_k(\infty)=\int_0^\infty j_k(t)\,\mathrm{d} t,\qquad C_L(\infty)+C_k(\infty)=1. \]

Substituting the characteristic solution into the boundary current gives

\[ C_L(\infty)=\frac1Z\int_0^\infty \partial_tF(L,t)w(L,t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \]

If \(k(\ell,t)=k(\ell)\), changing variables along each characteristic gives \(\mathrm d\xi=\mathrm d\eta/\nu(\eta)\) almost everywhere. The terminal probability is

\[ C_L(\infty)=\int_{-\infty}^L \frac{p_0(y,0)}{Z}\exp\!\left[-\int_y^L\frac{k(\eta)}{\nu(\eta)}\,\mathrm d\eta\right]\,\mathrm dy. \]

Without killing, \(w=1\) and \(j_L(t)=\partial_tF(L,t)/Z\), so \(C_L(\infty)=[1-F(L,0)]/Z=1\).

For multiple killing causes, \(C_L(\infty)+\sum_i C_i(\infty)=1\).

7. Application to BCOS in PRESS

Time to intercept and reference law

The function \(\tau(\ell)\) of the transport model is the time to intercept (TTI) of the carrier along the candidate route, and the killing is the interception of the carrier by the defenders. For a player \(j\) and a target location \(x\) on the pitch, the nominal TTI is

\[ \tau_j(x)=t_{\mathrm{turn},j}(x)+t_{\mathrm{move},j}(x), \]

the sum of the turning delay and the acceleration-limited travel time, both computed from the observed position and velocity of the player. The maximal speed and acceleration of the movement model are set separately for the carrier, who moves with the ball, and for the defenders. For a defender, \(\tau_j(x)\) is the nominal time to reach a potential interception location; for the carrier, it is the nominal time to reach \(x\). Following Spearman (Spearman et al. 2017; Spearman 2018), the uncertainty around the TTI is logistic, with arrival-time distribution and arrival hazard

\[ F_j(x,t)=\left[1+\exp\{-\kappa\,[t-\tau_j(x)]\}\right]^{-1},\qquad \lambda_j^{\mathrm{arr}}(t\mid x)=\frac{\partial_tF_j(x,t)}{1-F_j(x,t)}=\kappa F_j(x,t). \]

Here \(\kappa=\pi/(\sqrt{3}\,s)\), where \(s\) is the standard deviation of arrival-time uncertainty.

For a carrier initially at \(x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)\), the route is \(x(\ell;z)=x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)+\ell(z-x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0))/L\) for \(0\le\ell\le L\), with \(L=\|z-x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)\|\). Along this route, \(\tau(\ell)\) is the nominal TTI of the carrier at \(x(\ell;z)\), and \(F(\ell,t)\) is its logistic arrival-time distribution. Time is measured from the observed instant: the carrier first turns and then moves along the route. The extension to negative transport coordinates represents waiting at the observed origin, as specified below.

Transport velocity

The TTI of the carrier along a straight ray follows from the acceleration-limited movement model of PRESS. Let \(\ell\) denote distance along a straight ray from the carrier’s initial position. The observed speed is \(u\ge0\), and \(a>0\) and \(v_{\max}>0\) are the carrier’s acceleration and maximal speed. The angle \(\theta\) is fixed along the ray. The turning contribution is the constant

\[ t_{\mathrm{turn}}=\frac{u\theta}{a_{\mathrm{turn}}},\qquad a_{\mathrm{turn}}>0. \]

For \(u<v_{\max}\), set

\[ t_a=\frac{v_{\max}-u}{a},\qquad d_a=ut_a+\tfrac12at_a^2. \]

The function defining the transport is

\[ \tau(\ell)=t_{\mathrm{turn}}+\begin{cases} (\sqrt{u^2+2a\ell}-u)/a,&0\le \ell\le d_a,\\[3pt] t_a+(\ell-d_a)/v_{\max},&\ell>d_a. \end{cases} \]

For \(u\ge v_{\max}\), use \(\tau(\ell)=t_{\mathrm{turn}}+\ell/u\): the observed speed is maintained, including when it exceeds \(v_{\max}\). For the accelerating case,

\[ \tau'(\ell)=\begin{cases} 1/\sqrt{u^2+2a\ell},&0<\ell\le d_a,\\ 1/v_{\max},&\ell>d_a, \end{cases} \qquad \nu(\ell)=\begin{cases} \sqrt{u^2+2a\ell},&0<\ell\le d_a,\\ v_{\max},&\ell>d_a. \end{cases} \]

At \(d_a\), \(u^2+2ad_a=v_{\max}^2\), so \(\tau\) is \(C^1\).

Write \(t_{\mathrm{move}}(\ell)=\tau(\ell)-t_{\mathrm{turn}}\) for \(\ell\ge0\). Extend the movement time antisymmetrically:

\[ \boxed{\tau(\ell)=t_{\mathrm{turn}}+\begin{cases} -t_{\mathrm{move}}(-\ell),&\ell<0,\\ t_{\mathrm{move}}(\ell),&\ell\ge0. \end{cases}} \]

This extension is strictly increasing and tends to \(-\infty\) at the left end. For \(u>0\), it is \(C^1\) at zero, with \(\tau'(0)=1/u\). For \(u=0\), the derivative has an integrable singularity at zero; the velocity tends to zero from both sides. The transport-time coordinate specifies passage through zero, as in the characteristic construction above. The extension uses only the movement parameters already defined.

The inverse of the positive movement time is, for \(r\ge0\) and \(u<v_{\max}\),

\[ t_{\mathrm{move}}^{-1}(r)=\begin{cases} ur+\tfrac12ar^2,&0\le r\le t_a,\\ d_a+v_{\max}(r-t_a),&r>t_a. \end{cases} \]

For \(u\ge v_{\max}\), \(t_{\mathrm{move}}^{-1}(r)=ur\). Thus the extended inverse is

\[ \tau^{-1}(t)=\operatorname{sgn}(t-t_{\mathrm{turn}})\,t_{\mathrm{move}}^{-1}(|t-t_{\mathrm{turn}}|). \]

The constant turning delay cancels from the characteristic trajectories but affects the reference weights and initial conditioning. Negative coordinates are latent waiting states, whose physical location is the observed origin:

\[ x(\ell;z)=x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)+\frac{\max(\ell,0)}{L}(z-x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)),\qquad \ell\le L. \]

Since all negative coordinates share this location, the form of the extension is a convention.

For \(\tau(0)=t_{\mathrm{turn}}\ge0\), the initial physical position concentrates at the observed origin as \(\kappa\to\infty\): for \(0<\epsilon<L\), the conditioned probability of being beyond \(\epsilon\) is \([F(\epsilon,0)-F(L,0)]/Z\to0\). When the turning delay is positive, the latent reference median lies on the negative half-line, which maps to the origin.

Interception intensities

Interception occurs when a defender reaches the location occupied by the carrier while the carrier is still in possession. In pitch control, the arrival of each player is evaluated at every point of the ball trajectory, and each point represents an opportunity for interception. Here the arrival of each defender is evaluated in the same way at every point of the carrier’s route, and we model the local interception intensity by the defender’s arrival hazard at that location. Conditional on the carrier’s characteristic and the observed tracking state, the defenders’ interception processes are assumed independent; their intensities therefore add. For the route \(x(\ell;z)\), PRESS specifies the local killing intensity as

\[ k(\ell,t)=\sum_{i\in\mathrm{opp}} k_i(\ell,t),\qquad k_i(\ell,t)=\lambda_i^{\mathrm{arr}}\big(t\mid x(\ell;z)\big)=\kappa F_i(x(\ell;z),t). \]

On the negative half-line, the same intensity is evaluated at \(x_{\mathrm{bc}}(0)\), so interception can occur during waiting and turning. For a finite number of defenders, \(0\le k\le n_{\mathrm{opp}}\kappa\), satisfying the boundedness assumption above.

Accessibility and defender-specific risks

The accessibility probability is \(p_{\mathrm{bc}}(z)=C_L(\infty)\). The terminal incidence \(C_i(\infty)\) is the probability that defender \(i\) causes the first interception. Hence

\[ p_{\mathrm{bc}}(z)+\sum_{i\in\mathrm{opp}} C_i(\infty)=1. \]

These incidences quantify the contributions of individual defenders under the joint model. Evaluating \(p_{\mathrm{bc}}(z)\) across candidate destinations produces the BCOS accessibility surface.
