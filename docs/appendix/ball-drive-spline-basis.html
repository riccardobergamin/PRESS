Appendix: construction of the restricted cubic spline bases

This appendix describes the change of basis used for the nonlinear effects in the ball-drive risk model. The construction separates the constant and linear components, improves the empirical scaling and conditioning of each nonlinear block, and diagonalizes its curvature penalty. The resulting penalty can be expressed through independent Gaussian priors on the transformed coefficients. The benchmark describes the selection of the smoothing strengths.

1. Normalization and knots

For one covariate \(x\), let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be the fitting values. Normalize them using their fitting-set range,

\[ z_i=\frac{x_i-x_{\min}}{x_{\max}-x_{\min}}. \]

The \(K\) knots \(\kappa_1<\cdots<\kappa_K\) are empirical quantiles of \(z\). PRESS uses \(K=5\) for carrier speed and boundary distance, and \(K=4\) for the approach angle. The quantile levels are \(0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1\) and \(0,1/3,2/3,1\), respectively, so \(\kappa_1=0\) and \(\kappa_K=1\). There are \(q=K-2\) nonlinear basis functions: three for speed and boundary distance, and two for angle.

All knots, normalizations and transformations below are determined from the fitting data and reused for new observations. Angle values are restricted to observations with a valid direction.

2. Restricted cubic basis

Define \(t_j(z)=(z-\kappa_j)_+^3\), where \((u)_+=\max(u,0)\). For \(j=1,\ldots,K-2\), let

\[ c_j(z)=t_j(z) -\frac{\kappa_K-\kappa_j}{\kappa_K-\kappa_{K-1}}\,t_{K-1}(z) +\frac{\kappa_{K-1}-\kappa_j}{\kappa_K-\kappa_{K-1}}\,t_K(z). \]

These combinations are piecewise cubic between knots and linear beyond the boundary knots. A general function in the spline space can be written as

\[ f(z)=a_0+a_1z+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta_jc_j(z). \]

Here \(\boldsymbol\delta\) denotes coefficients in the raw basis; \(\boldsymbol\gamma\) below denotes coefficients in the final transformed basis. The change of basis preserves the represented spline space when the constant and linear components are retained separately.

This is a generic representation in the normalized coordinate \(z\); the fitted linear speed and boundary terms retain the original covariate units, as detailed below.

3. Orthogonalization against the constant and linear terms

Let \(C_{ij}=c_j(z_i)\) and \(L=[\mathbf 1,z]\). Remove the least-squares projection onto the constant and linear design,

\[ A=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_A\|C-LA\|_F^2, \qquad E=C-LA, \qquad L^\top E=0. \]

For full-column-rank \(L\),

\[ A=(L^\top L)^{-1}L^\top C. \]

Thus, \(E\) represents nonlinear deviations that are empirically orthogonal to the separate constant and linear terms. Separating linear and nonlinear components is a standard motivation for reparameterizing penalized spline effects; see Scheipl, Fahrmeir, and Kneib (2012, sec. 2.1). The matrix \(A\) is retained for evaluating the same transformation on new observations.

Since \(x=x_{\min}+(x_{\max}-x_{\min})z\), the designs \([\mathbf 1,x]\) and \([\mathbf 1,z]\) span the same space. Residualization against \([\mathbf 1,z]\) therefore also removes the projection onto a linear effect expressed in the original units of \(x\).

4. Empirical whitening

The Gram matrix of the residualized basis is

\[ G=\frac{E^\top E}{n}. \]

Assuming \(G\) is positive definite, write \(G=U\Lambda U^\top\) and define

\[ W=U\Lambda^{-1/2}, \qquad E_w=EW. \]

Then

\[ \frac1n E_w^\top E_w =W^\top GW =I_q. \]

The columns are orthonormal under the empirical inner product on the fitting set. Because they are already centered by the projection onto \(L\), they also have unit empirical variance. This removes scale differences and empirical correlations within the nonlinear block, improving its numerical conditioning. It is the PCA-whitening transformation written for observations arranged in rows (Kessy, Lewin, and Strimmer 2018).

5. Curvature penalty and rotation

Define the curvature matrix with derivatives taken with respect to the normalized coordinate \(z\),

\[ R_{jk}=\int_0^1 c_j''(z)c_k''(z)\,dz. \]

Subtracting a constant and a linear function leaves second derivatives unchanged, so residualization does not change this curvature matrix. The second derivatives \(c_j''\) vanish outside \([\kappa_1,\kappa_K]=[0,1]\), so the integral over \([0,1]\) covers the whole line. The integral is evaluated over consecutive knot intervals. Within each interval the second derivatives are linear and their products are quadratic; two-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature therefore evaluates these integrals exactly, apart from floating-point rounding.

Diagonalize the penalty in the whitened coordinates,

\[ W^\top RW =V\operatorname{diag}(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_q)V^\top, \qquad V^\top V=I_q. \]

Set \(T=WV\). The final basis is \(\widetilde E=ET\) and satisfies

\[ \frac1n\widetilde E^\top\widetilde E=I_q, \qquad T^\top RT=\operatorname{diag}(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_q). \]

With the row vector \(\boldsymbol c(z)=[c_1(z),\ldots,c_q(z)]\), the final basis functions evaluated at \(z\) are

\[ \widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)=\bigl[\boldsymbol c(z)-[1,z]A\bigr]T, \]

and the nonlinear component of the covariate effect is \(f_{\mathrm{nonlinear}}(z)=\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)\boldsymbol\gamma\), where \(\boldsymbol\gamma\) are the coefficients in the final basis. The components of \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)\) are the nonlinear basis functions \(b_{jk}\) of the model vignette, for covariate \(j\). The rotation preserves empirical orthonormality while making the curvature penalty diagonal. This is a Demmler–Reinsch-type change of basis (Demmler and Reinsch 1975); an explicit matrix derivation of simultaneous design and penalty diagonalization is also given by Lang et al. (2012, sec. 4).

6. Independent Gaussian priors in the transformed basis

For nonlinear coefficients \(\boldsymbol\gamma\) in the final basis, the integrated squared curvature is

\[ \int_0^1\left[f_{\mathrm{nonlinear}}''(z)\right]^2\,dz =\boldsymbol\gamma^\top T^\top RT\boldsymbol\gamma =\sum_{j=1}^{q}\eta_j\gamma_j^2. \]

PRESS normalizes the eigenvalues by their minimum,

\[ \omega_j=\frac{\eta_j}{\min_k\eta_k}, \qquad \Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_q). \]

For a fixed smoothing strength \(\rho>0\), this gives the independent Gaussian priors

\[ \gamma_j\sim\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac1{\rho\omega_j}\right), \]

with negative log density

\[ -\log p(\boldsymbol\gamma\mid\rho) =\frac{\rho}{2}\boldsymbol\gamma^\top\Omega\boldsymbol\gamma +\text{constant} =\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_j\gamma_j^2 +\text{constant}. \]

The constant is independent of \(\boldsymbol\gamma\) for fixed \(\rho\) and basis. Directions with greater curvature receive stronger shrinkage. Dividing by the smallest eigenvalue is a normalization convention: it rescales the overall curvature penalty while preserving its relative weights. The diagonal representation simplifies the prior specification, and the coefficients remain dependent in the posterior.

7. Linear terms in the original units

In the fitted PRESS model, the carrier effects are parameterized as

\[ g_v(v_{\mathrm{bc}})=\beta_v v_{\mathrm{bc}} +\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}_v(z_v)\boldsymbol\gamma_v, \qquad g_B(B)=\beta_B B +\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}_B(z_B)\boldsymbol\gamma_B. \]

Thus, \(\beta_v\) multiplies speed in m/s and \(\beta_B\) multiplies boundary distance in m; min–max normalization is used internally for the nonlinear bases. The intercept \(\alpha=\log\lambda_0\) remains a separate model parameter. The priors and preset initial values motivated by the preliminary log-linear model refer to these original-unit linear coefficients.

For comparison, after the nonlinear change of basis, the same one-covariate function could be written in either coordinate as

\[ \alpha_x+\beta_x x+\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)\boldsymbol\gamma =a_0^*+a_1^*z+\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)\boldsymbol\gamma, \]

where \(a_0^*=\alpha_x+\beta_x x_{\min}\) and \(a_1^*=\beta_x(x_{\max}-x_{\min})\). These are equivalent functions with different linear coefficients. Preserving the prior under this reparameterization would require transforming its joint distribution as well; independent standard normal priors on \(a_0^*\) and \(a_1^*\) would define a different prior. PRESS retains the original-unit parameterization for speed and boundary distance. The linear approach-angle term is treated separately: it multiplies the angle standardized by its fitting-set mean and standard deviation, as specified in the model vignette.

8. Evaluation on new observations

For a new value \(x\), use the fitting-set normalization \(z=(x-x_{\min})/(x_{\max}-x_{\min})\) and evaluate \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol c}(z)\) as defined in Section 5. The fitting-set matrices \(A\) and \(T\) are unchanged. Orthogonality and unit empirical variance hold on the fitting set and can differ on a different sample. Values outside the fitting range are evaluated using the spline’s linear tails.
