4.1 · Use with other providers: data and preparation

This is part 4.1 of the User guide. PRESS evaluates a match in a provider-independent representation. Original SkillCorner files can be loaded directly with PRESS.from_skillcorner(...). For another provider, map identifiers and coordinates explicitly, align events to tracking, then supply or reconstruct individual possessions. PRESS constructs canonical actions and checkpoints from those intervals. See the Köln–Bayern example.

Tracking and match metadata

Positions use metres, centred at the pitch centre: x is longitudinal and y lateral. Velocities use m/s in those same axes. PRESS keeps the supplied direction, so state the ball carrier’s attacking direction at each frame. fps is the input sampling frequency; the models’ numerical integration steps remain fixed.

Information Canonical fields Requirement and consequence of absence
Match and sampling match_id (string), fps (positive float) Match identifier is optional for isolated inference. Correct fps is mandatory for segmentation, elapsed time and checkpoint spacing.
Pitch pitch_length, pitch_width (positive metres) Supply actual dimensions; the constructor retains its 105 × 68 m default when omitted.
Frame key frame (unique integer) Mandatory. Consecutive IDs must represent consecutive samples; gaps must retain their missing IDs.
Period period (integer/string identifier) Mandatory for segmentation and period-relative synchronization. Period means a half or an extra-time period.
Player identity home_<integer> or away_<integer> Mandatory, stable within match. Numbers are aliases that may differ from shirt numbers. Preserve provider IDs in player metadata.
Player state <player>_x, _y, _vx, _vy (numeric) Observed players require finite position/velocity. Absent players remain missing; PRESS pads numerical states internally. At most eleven observed players per team.
Carrier player_possession (alias or missing) Mandatory to evaluate a frame. Must be annotated or estimated explicitly upstream, since PRESS takes the carrier from this field.
Direction attacking_side: left_to_right or right_to_left Mandatory for the contextual models and field geometry. Reliable recovery requires information beyond a single frame.
Ball ball_x, ball_y; optional ball_z XY required by the match interface and real-frame plots. Z is needed for observed 3D launch fitting in training; production field predictions use XY only.
Clock and score match_clock_min, score_difference_prefix Required contextual information for a complete observed feature record. Numeric missing values are accepted by the fitted trees, and differ from known values. Score difference is carrier-team minus opposition, using only prior confirmed goals.
Player metadata player_id, optional source IDs, name, goalkeeper flag Optional for physical inference; role is needed to reproduce outfield-only evaluation cohorts. Take role from provider metadata or annotations.

For long data, use convert_long_tracking(players, ball, context, home_team=..., away_team=...). Player rows require frame, team_id, player_id, x, y, vx, vy; ball rows require frame, x, y; context requires frame, player_possession, attacking_side. team_possession disambiguates carrier IDs reused between teams. The returned roster records the alias mapping.

map_columns(table, {source: canonical}) renames fields. to_pitch_coordinates(...) converts declared coordinate bounds and optional velocity components. Both rely on the units and orientation declared by the caller. differentiate(...) is available when velocities must be reconstructed: derivatives are calculated separately on finite contiguous runs within each period. The filtering window must be stated by the caller.

Event information

normalize_events accepts a DataFrame with unique event_id, event_type, and either frame, UTC-compatible datetime, or period plus time_seconds. A supplied mapping translates provider event labels to the canonical event vocabulary. Unsupported event types become unknown; their source label is retained in original_event.

Event/attribute Needed for Missing information
Actor and team: player_id, team_id Release, recovery and terminal action identification Actorless administrative events are retained as administrative entries of the preprocessing audit.
Time and period; optional event XY Event–tracking synchronization Time links alone provide nearest-timestamp matching; release/contact refinement uses event XY and type-specific tracking information through an external synchronizer.
pass, is_successful (nullable boolean), receiver_player_id Pass release, successful reception and verified pass links Missing success stays unknown. A successful pass without an identified, observed receiver has an unverified endpoint.
recovery, interception, tackle; annotated winner where available Control origin and direct possession loss An annotated control acquisition can establish a start. Contact or a change of estimated controller alone does not establish a verified loss. Provider-specific tackle outcomes must identify what was won and by whom.
restart_type: open_play, throw_in, corner, free_kick, goal_kick, unknown Reception/interception context Reconstruct from the preceding pass and its restart qualifier. If unavailable, use unknown.
shot Terminal action and shot-ending cohort exclusion Without shot labels, the published no-shot cohort cannot be reproduced.
foul_suffered, clearance Separate noncanonical terminal actions Both remain terminal actions separate from individual loss. Omission can leave the endpoint unknown.
stoppage, restart Prevent linking across interrupted play Missing interruption information weakens endpoint verification and should be reported.
OBE and reliable endpoint annotations Reproduce the published outcome-validation filters Providers supply these in varying degree, and the outcome labels must come from annotated on-ball engagement or validated loss outcomes.

The public raw context categories are pass_reception, pass_interception, recovery, keep_possession, and reception/interception variants prefixed with throw_in, corner, free_kick, or goal_kick. ball_drive is additionally a previous-action category. unknown is accepted by the unchanged trained categorical vocabularies. The adapter infers keep_possession only with supporting annotation. Other unseen categories use the fitted unseen bucket.

Synchronization and segmentation

The separate synchronize_events(...) helper can validate supplied frame links or find nearest timestamps within periods. Event-specific release/contact refinement remains a separate step. Keep provider loading and alignment in the external preprocessing workflow: synchronization and individual-possession boundaries may be prepared outside PRESS, and canonical action segmentation follows the PRESS convention. Tracking and events must share canonical identifiers and synchronized frame links before segmentation; the segmenter verifies the links, and time alignment happens upstream.

For already prepared data, initialize PRESS(tracking_data, ...) directly. Supplied individual possessions are used as they are: initialize PRESS(tracking, player_possessions_data=individuals, ...) without actions_data, and PRESS constructs the canonical action table and checkpoints internally with canonical_actions(...). If individual intervals are missing, call segment_possessions(tracking, events, fps=..., match_id=...) first. It returns (tracking, individual_possessions, actions, report); pass the tracking and individual-possession tables to the constructor.

The segmenter uses synchronized actor events and the supplied carrier stream. Direct event links establish the eligible starts and terminal actions; clock-interpolated audit links do not establish these boundaries. A reception is located at an observed control frame or, when control is missed, at the fallback candidate described below, and each pass link is cross-checked against estimated control and flagged with outcome_verified. include_unannotated_starts=False restricts starts to successful receptions and annotated recoveries/interceptions, as in the IDSSE example. The default True additionally admits tracking-only starts and opponent control after failed passes; their boundary source is recorded.

A missed receiver control can use the frame immediately before the receiver’s next synchronized actor event when the observed ball–receiver XY distance is at most reception_fallback_max_distance (default 1.5 m). The candidate must exist in the same period and strictly follow release; unavailable coordinates or an inconsistent distance cannot support the fallback. Set the parameter to None to disable it. reception_fallback_checks records the tested candidates and reasons.

A recovery/interception overlapping the outgoing carrier’s tackle loss is reconciled only when winner/loser identities and adjacent relevant events support the same transfer. The outgoing end remains at the synchronized tackle frame and the incoming start moves to the next observed frame in that period. Original frames and event IDs remain recorded in overlap_reconciliations and the interval table. Other overlaps are reported without creating another interval.

The resulting intervals do not overlap or cross periods and missing tracking ranges. Terminal events preserve their football meaning: a foul suffered or clearance differs from direct loss, and a change of estimated controller without an eligible terminal event leaves the ending unknown. Original estimated control is retained separately from the carrier assigned to each reconstructed interval.

Provider annotations determine which events can support these labels. The IDSSE segmentation rules specify the starts, the reception search, the terminal events and tackle-winner rule, foul deduplication, clearance qualifier, pass-link verification and unknown-outcome handling for the Bundesliga example. Other providers need an equivalent semantic mapping, and each event meaning must be supported by the provider’s annotations.

Actions follow the existing canonical segmentation conventions: successful whole one-second carries; a failed final carry even when shorter than one second; and a pass-release checkpoint. Short successful tails are noncanonical exposure snapshots. Foul, clearance and shot endings remain outside the terminal pass/carry choices. previous_action uses the control’s origin before the first whole carry, then ball_drive. time_since_start is computed from actual frame differences and fps.

Keep the external event audit and segmentation report alongside the input tables. The direct constructor receives prepared tracking, individual intervals and metadata; an action table is optional. The original SkillCorner loader additionally exposes its raw dynamic-event audit through get_preprocessing_events() and its loading report through get_preparation_report(). Export input/audit tables separately from numerical result exports.

Requirements by model

Calculation Minimum information beyond its fitted artifact
Ball-drive risk Carrier and defender positions/velocities, native pitch dimensions. Inference needs the state only.
Pitch control/pass probability Team identity, player positions/velocities, carrier and candidate destinations. The production flight model works from the current state.
Pass selection The physical fields, xT, carrier state and contextual features. Missing categorical information has explicit fitted buckets.
BCOS Carrier and defender positions/velocities; pitch dimensions and chosen destination domain.
Action selection and h Preceding fields/choices, BCOS model feature, context and completed-carry count. The count needs a control start or an explicit supplied value.
Residual risk and xLoss The same state plus action selection and observed elapsed individual-control time. Only past and current information is used.
Published validation/report cohorts In addition: matched control endpoints, identified pass links, terminal action/outcome, interruptions, role and OBE where required. Missing annotations prevent reproducing that evaluation cohort.

Interoperability means that the same mathematical models can evaluate a mapped state; calibration and predictive validity on a new league, provider or segmentation system require separate evidence.

IDSSE preprocessing

The standalone preprocessing scripts load Sportec metadata and map provider semantics. The DataBallPy bridge runs separately from PRESS and exports portable Parquet tables. It was executed with DataBallPy 0.7.3; the bridge checks that API version explicitly. Other synchronizers can supply the same canonical interface.

The example uses event-anchored starts (include_unannotated_starts=False), DataBallPy’s event-order-preserving alignment and its tracking-based control estimate. Its control parameters are explicit: 1.5 m possession zone, 5 m/s ball-speed threshold, 10° ball-angle threshold and zero minimum possession-zone frames. Unsupported events may receive a separately labelled clock interpolation only between nearby aligned anchors in one period. A synchronization certainty score is an uncalibrated indicator. The resulting possessions are tracking-based estimates.
